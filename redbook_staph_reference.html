Invasive Staphylococcal Infections

Concise teaching excerpt adapted for the Meridian simulation from the American Academy of Pediatrics Red Book discussion of invasive Staphylococcus aureus infection.

Key treatment principle

For serious infection caused by methicillin-susceptible S. aureus (MSSA), use an antistaphylococcal beta-lactam such as cefazolin, nafcillin, or oxacillin when susceptibility is established or strongly supported.

How to use the microbiology

  • A rapid blood-culture identification test that detects S. aureus and does not detect mecA/C supports presumptive MSSA while final susceptibilities are pending.
  • Choice of empiric therapy should also account for illness severity and local resistance patterns.
  • Final culture identification and susceptibility results should still be reviewed when available.

Why spectrum matters

  • Vancomycin is appropriate when MRSA is suspected or confirmed, but it is not preferred definitive therapy for MSSA when a beta-lactam can be used.
  • Targeted therapy improves drug exposure against MSSA and avoids unnecessary broad-spectrum treatment.
