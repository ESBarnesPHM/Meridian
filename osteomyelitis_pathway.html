Pediatric Osteomyelitis: Evaluation & Management

Suspect osteoarticular infection
Persistent fever, focal pain, refusal to bear weight, abnormal examination, or failure to follow the expected course.
↓
Initial evaluation
CBC, CRP, blood culture before antibiotics when clinically safe, targeted imaging, and Orthopedic consultation when joint involvement, collection, or source control is a concern.
↓
Unstable / toxic
Begin prompt empiric therapy, evaluate for sepsis, and escalate level of care. Do not delay treatment for imaging.
Stable
Obtain diagnostic studies and use imaging, microbiology, and local susceptibility data to guide treatment.
↓
MRI confirms osteomyelitis
Assess for septic arthritis, drainable abscess, subperiosteal collection, and need for operative source control.
↓
Select antibiotic therapy
Integrate Gram stain, rapid organism/resistance testing, local antibiogram, allergy history, illness severity, and source-control plan.
↓
Monitor response
Fever curve, pain, perfusion, oral intake, urine output, mobility, examination, and CRP trend. Reassess source control or diagnosis if the course worsens or fails to improve.
